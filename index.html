🎓 THESIS

Automated Detection of Diabetic Retinopathy in Retinal Images Using Deep Learning

Thesis Description

Developed and implemented advanced deep learning architectures, including MobileNetV3 and EfficientNet B0, along with Vision Transformers for automated retinal image analysis.

MobileNetV3 achieved 98.91% accuracy, while EfficientNet B0 achieved 98.1% accuracy.

The study successfully performed binary and multiclass classification tasks to detect and evaluate specific severity stages of Diabetic Retinopathy.

Explainable AI (XAI) techniques were also integrated for visual decision-mapping to improve clinical reliability.

ENGINEERING CASE STUDY 3 min read
DEEP LEARNING & EDGE AI

Automated Diabetic Retinopathy Detection & Clinical Severity Grading Under Edge Constraints

Lightweight CNN architectures and Vision Transformers benchmarked on 103,020 fundus images for resource-constrained diagnostics.

01. Problem Statement

Diabetic Retinopathy (DR) is one of the leading causes of preventable blindness globally. Early diagnosis requires direct evaluation by trained ophthalmologists via fundus imaging. However, rural clinics and resource-limited health centers face acute shortages of specialists and dedicated high-performance computing clusters.

Most state-of-the-art vision models require hundreds of megabytes of GPU memory, rendering automated edge deployment in mobile screening vans and embedded devices impractical.

02. Engineering Constraints

  • Inference Latency: Must run near real-time on edge devices (under 100 ms per sample).
  • Model Footprint: Target memory under 25 MB without sacrificing diagnostic sensitivity.
  • Clinical Class Imbalance: Heavily skewed datasets across early non-proliferative versus advanced proliferative stages.
  • Clinical Transparency: Black-box predictions are unacceptable in medical triage; pixel-level attribution is mandatory.

03. System & Pipeline Architecture

The dataset was expanded to 103,020 augmented retinal fundus images using contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalization (CLAHE) and geometric transforms. The pipeline evaluates both lightweight convolutional neural networks and transformer backbones:

MobileNetV3-Large

Hard-swish activations & inverted residual bottlenecks for binary screening (~5.4M params).

EfficientNet-B0

Compound coefficient scaling balancing depth, width, and resolution for 5-stage grading.

Explainable AI (XAI)

Grad-CAM and LIME heatmaps integrated to highlight microaneurysms and hemorrhages for clinician verification.

04. Final Results & Metrics

Architecture Parameters Val Accuracy Test Accuracy Target Task
MobileNetV3-Large ~5.4M 98.91% 98.50% Binary Detection
EfficientNet-B0 ~5.3M 98.10% 98.30% 5-Stage Severity
Vision Transformer (ViT-Base) ~86.0M 96.40% 95.80% Multiclass Benchmark

MobileNetV3-Large surpassed heavy transformer alternatives on resource-constrained binary tasks, delivering an ideal balance of compute efficiency and accuracy for real-world deployment.
